1. The plot in 9 lines

Ch.What happensHer annotation focus
1Nick moves to West Egg, dinner at Tom & Daisy's in East Egg. Tom's racism, his mistress calls. Daisy: "beautiful little fool." Ends with Gatsby reaching for the green light.Characterization, setting
2Valley of Ashes, Eckleburg's eyes. Tom takes Nick to meet Myrtle (wife of George Wilson). NYC apartment party; Tom breaks Myrtle's nose for saying "Daisy."How Tom and Nick change; what you think of Nick
3Gatsby's party. Guests uninvited, wild; rumors (killed a man, German spy). Owl Eyes + real-but-uncut books. Nick meets Gatsby ("old sport"). Jordan cheated at golf. Nick: "one of the few honest people."Party guests, what the party is like, rumors
4Gatsby's life story (San Francisco/"Middle West", Oxford, war medal). Lunch with Wolfsheim (fixed 1919 World Series, molar cufflinks). Jordan reveals Gatsby + Daisy loved each other in 1917 Louisville; he bought the house to be across from her; wants Nick to invite Daisy to tea.Wolfsheim; what Gatsby reveals; what Jordan reveals
5The reunion at Nick's. Rain → sun. Gatsby nearly breaks the clock. "Acting like a little boy." Gatsby glowing. House tour, Daisy cries over the shirts. Green light becomes "just a light." "Colossal vitality of his illusion."Weather/mood, the clock, Gatsby's demeanor
6Truth: James Gatz, poor North Dakota farm kid, reinvented himself at 17; Dan Cody. Tom & Daisy come to a party; Daisy is appalled, Tom calls him a bootlegger. "Can't repeat the past? Why of course you can!"Truth about Gatsby, Dan Cody, Tom & Daisy's reaction, expectations vs. reality
7Climax. Hottest day. Parties stop. "You always look so cool" → Tom knows. Car swap. Plaza Hotel fight; Tom exposes bootlegging; Daisy won't say she never loved Tom. Daisy driving the yellow car kills Myrtle; Gatsby will take the blame.Heat, carpooling, conflict, responsibility
8Gatsby tells Nick the real Louisville story. "You're worth the whole damn bunch put together." Wilson: "God sees everything" (Eckleburg). Wilson shoots Gatsby in the pool, then himself.Disillusionment, responsibility, Gatsby the next morning
9Nobody comes to the funeral except Henry Gatz (dad, with the boyhood schedule) and Owl Eyes. Tom & Daisy left. Tom admits he told Wilson. "Careless people." Nick goes home. "Boats against the current."Henry Gatz, where everyone ends up, Nick on NY

2. The 8 characters on her study guide

WhoMust-know
Jay GatsbyProtagonist. Real name James Gatz. New money (bootlegging). Throws parties to attract Daisy. Believes he can repeat the past. Dies in his pool.
Nick CarrawayFirst-person narrator, Daisy's cousin, West Egg, bond business. Claims he doesn't judge (he does) → possibly unreliable. "Within and without."
Daisy BuchananOld money, East Egg, wears white, voice "full of money." Loved Gatsby in 1917, married Tom. Kills Myrtle, lets Gatsby take the blame, leaves.
Tom BuchananAntagonist. Old money, brute, racist, cheats with Myrtle. Exposes Gatsby at the Plaza. Tells Wilson the yellow car is Gatsby's.
Jordan BakerDaisy's friend, golfer, dishonest (cheated), Nick's love interest. Tells Nick the 1917 story. "Bad driver."
Myrtle WilsonTom's mistress, wants to climb socially. Killed by the yellow car.
George WilsonMyrtle's husband, garage in the Valley of Ashes. Poor, worn out. Kills Gatsby then himself.
Meyer WolfsheimGangster, fixed the 1919 World Series, molar cufflinks, "gonnegtion." Gatsby's money source. Won't come to the funeral.

3. Symbols on her study guide

SymbolMeans
Green lightGatsby's dream / Daisy / the American Dream. Loses magic once he has her (Ch. 5). Last page: Dutch sailors' "fresh, green breast of the new world."
WhitePurity/innocence on the surface (Daisy, Jordan) — fake.
Yellow / goldWealth and its corruption. Yellow car = death car.
CarsWealth + carelessness/recklessness. Jordan the bad driver, the party crash, Myrtle's death.
Daisy's voice"Full of money" — her charm is wealth.
Gatsby's booksReal but uncut (never read) — Gatsby's image is carefully built, looks real, has no depth.
Eckleburg's eyesGod watching a morally empty world. "God sees everything." / "That's an advertisement."
Valley of AshesThe poor, gray waste left behind by the rich.
The clock (Ch. 5)Gatsby trying to stop time / repeat the past.

4. Literary terms (the 13 general questions)

TermQuick definition + Gatsby example
ToneNarrator's/author's attitude. Nick on Tom & Daisy at the end = bitter.
MoodWhat the reader feels. Valley of Ashes = bleak. Ch. 7 heat = tense.
SymbolismAn object standing for an idea (green light).
Verbal ironySaying the opposite of what you mean. Nick: "one of the few honest people I have ever known."
Dramatic ironyReader knows what a character doesn't. Tom/Wilson think Gatsby drove the car; we know Daisy did.
Situational ironyOpposite of what you'd expect. Hundreds at his parties, almost nobody at his funeral.
Topic vs. themeTopic = a word ("wealth"). Theme = a full statement ("Wealth does not bring true happiness").
Protagonist / antagonistGatsby / Tom.
Point of viewFirst person (Nick), looking back.
CharacterizationDirect (told) vs. indirect (STEAL: Speech, Thoughts, Effect on others, Actions, Looks).
Plot diagramExposition (Ch. 1-3) → rising action (Ch. 4-6) → climax Ch. 7 (Plaza / Myrtle's death) → falling action (Ch. 8) → resolution (Ch. 9).

6. Themes (from her essay prompt)

  • Wealth does not bring true happiness
  • The American Dream is not fully attainable
  • Greed leads to loneliness
  • Moral carelessness leads to corruption
  • Appearances often defy reality
